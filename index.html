The brief

How might we let an AI agent turn one conversation about the family into a cohesive weekend plan of travel, stay and activities that the parent trusts enough to approve and book?

4 days. 3 families. 1 AI tested. 1 challenge: reimagine Myra so it plans around everyone — and leaves the parent in charge of what gets locked in.

Razorpay asked for an AI travel planning experience that takes someone from intent to booking for a multi-day trip — flights, stays and activities as one plan they can review, tweak and approve, going beyond search and filter.

The problem

Myra plans for the parent, not with them.

I tested MakeMyTrip's Myra with a real family scenario: a weekend in Pondicherry with a motion-sick 3-year-old, two grandparents and a fixed budget. It forgot what I'd told it, decided without confirming, and never showed its working — so I had to check everything myself.

    So the redesign checks every suggestion against the family first — and leaves the parent in charge of what gets locked in.

    Who I designed for

    Three kinds of planners. I chose the one whose trip is hardest to get right.

    A family trip has the most needs pulling against each other — a child's routine, grandparents' pace, one budget. That's where an AI agent can do more than search and filter.

          Solution

          Myra now plans with the parent: it confirms what it heard, checks every option in the open, and lets the parent decide.

          Same Myra, same chat, same MakeMyTrip — reimagined around three moments where the parent used to be left out.

            + Start anywhere

            Three ways in, one plan.

            After the family is confirmed, the parent picks where to start — places, an itinerary or travel. A checklist keeps track, and Myra suggests what's next. Asked for an itinerary too early, Myra drafts an honest rough one with open slots.

            How AI works

            Myra does the legwork. The parent makes the call.

            Each part of the redesign answers one way the parent was left out of today's Myra.

            1. Current : It forgets what it was told

              It remembers and shows what it's using

              “Here's what I understood” after every answer, removable pills, a pinned plan checklist and a “Planning around” strip carried into every step.

            2. Current : It decides without asking

              It asks first, then waits

              Confirm points before planning (trip, then family), Skip for any question, three ways to start, and nothing locked in until the parent says so.

            3. Current: It doesn't show its working

              It informs its working and its gaps

              “Checked against” on every option, left-out options with reasons, estimates labelled “est.”, and honest gaps: “I don't know your places yet.”

            Myra does the legwork

            • Finds options and checks each against every person
            • Explains why — and what it left out
            • Keeps track of the plan across sessions

            The parent makes the call

            • Confirms what Myra heard, or edits it
            • Chooses where to start and what to keep
            • Decides what gets locked in, and when

            What's next

            What I'd do with more time.

            1. 01

              Cover the other edge cases

              Only over budget is prototyped. Next: too many beaches, nothing saved, prices that don't load, and a family member's needs changing mid-plan.

            2. 02

              Prototype the full booking

              Book hotel and cab in one flow: one total, clear cancellation, what happens if one booking fails, and a plan to share with the family.

            3. 03

              Test with families

              Put the prototype in front of the three families I spoke to.

            4. 04

              Measure trust

              Fewer exits to YouTube and hotel sites; fewer over-budget surprises.
