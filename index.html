Planning a family trip shouldn't mean planning around everyone, alone.

Myra, MakeMyTrip's AI assistant, redesigned to plan a weekend around a 3-year-old, two grandparents and a ₹45K budget — through conversation.

Starts where families already are

Myra sits in the MakeMyTrip app's nav bar. One tap and the parent describes the trip in their own words.

Plans around every person

Needs for each person are asked once, confirmed with the parent, and checked against every suggestion.

Shows evidence, not a pamphlet

Fit tags with reasons, honest gaps, and options left out — with why — so the parent can trust the plan.

Who I designed for

Three kinds of planners. I chose the one whose trip is hardest to get right.

A family trip has the most needs pulling against each other — a child's routine, grandparents' pace, one budget. That's where an AI agent can do more than search and filter.

  • How they plan

    Google, YouTube and now AI. Trusts other travellers more than listings.

  • How they book

    Across 4+ platforms — whichever they trust for that part of the trip.

  • What they need

    A plan that respects everyone's routine and limits, without checking it all themselves.

  • What frustrates them

    Listings that don't match reality, plans scattered across apps, surprises at checkout.

Questions & investigations

I asked three planners to walk me through the last family trip they actually took.

15–20 minute conversations about a real trip — not what they'd want, but what they did, step by step.

What I wanted to find out

  1. 01How do families decide where, when and how they'll travel?
  2. 02What do they check before booking and for whom?
  3. 03Where do they book each part, and why there?
  4. 04How does the plan change between the first idea and the trip?
  5. 05Where does AI help today, and where does it fail?
